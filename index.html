Wenchao Yue

I'm a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Electronic Engineering at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, where I build soft robots and tactile sensors for minimally invasive surgery. I'm co-supervised by Hongliang Ren, Wu (Scott) Yuan and Cecilia Laschi.

Before CUHK, I received my M.Sc. (with distinction) in Mechanical Engineering from the National University of Singapore in 2021, advised by Francis E. H. Tay. At NUS I was a research engineer at the Advanced Robotics Centre with Prof. Laschi, and a research assistant in the Medical Mechatronics Lab (Biomedical Engineering) with Prof. Ren.

Research

I'm interested in soft robotics, tactile sensing and surgical robot autonomy. Much of my work is about soft pop-up machines that fold perception and actuation into one thin module — thermo-pneumatic pouch motors, balloon actuators with embodied sensing, origami and kirigami structures — and about multimodal tactile interfaces (OCT-based, piezoelectric and vision-based) that let minimally invasive surgical robots feel tissue mechanics and decide where to act, e.g. Bayesian active palpation for incision recommendation in tracheotomy and for lesion profiling inside luminal organs. My recent work is supported by the Hong Kong RGC Collaborative Research Fund (CRF) and Research Impact Fund (RIF) on multimodal visual–tactile learning for surgical robots.
